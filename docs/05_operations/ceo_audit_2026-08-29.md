# Executive audit — 2026-08-29

Full-bench audit of Mise en Place by the Open Executive virtual executive team: six
specialist consults (Strategy, Finance, Product, Marketing, Operations, Legal), each
operating under its domain prompt, reading this repository — the June 2026 business plan,
the [27-Aug beta-readiness review](./beta_readiness_review_2026-08-27.md), the product,
marketing, engineering and operations docs, the ADRs, and the source — then synthesized
by the Executive persona.

Context at audit time: pre-revenue, solo technical founder, private beta imminent,
raising €750k.

## Verdict

The engineering discipline here is well above solo-founder norm — 31 ADRs, 241 test
files, CI that hard-fails on skipped DB suites, tenant isolation enforced by lint gates
and an RLS backstop, and a QA pass that drove every screen in a real browser. The core
product is real: photo → confidence-scored extraction → guided review → price-shock
alerts attacks a margin-level pain with working code behind it.

**The problem is not readiness. It is that the company currently tells three different
stories about itself, and one of them is told by a legal entity that does not exist.**

- The investor plan prices at €49/€99/€199; the shipped code prices at €29/€59/€129.
- The plan sells payment reminders and RD 238/2026 payment-state reporting as core
  capabilities; on 27 August we correctly killed the payment model.
- The privacy policy and terms name "Mise en Place SL" as data controller; no SL is
  registered.

None of these is fatal. All are the same disease: the product moved and the paper did
not. Coherence is a solo founder's scarcest asset in front of investors and testers
alike, and it is entirely within our control to fix in two weeks.

## The three variables that decide this

1. **Does a gestoría actually prescribe this?** The channel carries 30%→60% of projected
   signups, the CAC ≤ €250 math, and 35% of the raise — and the marketing docs admit
   nobody has spoken to a single despacho. Five interviews cost two afternoons and either
   validate the model or reset it. → #783
2. **Does the review habit survive week 4?** Every roadmap item — PO matching, gestoría
   portal, e-invoice module — is worthless if an owner-chef stops photographing albaranes
   once the novelty fades. Nothing measures this today. → #786
3. **Can we present one coherent story by the time anyone looks?** One price table, one
   regulatory claim set, one controller of record. Inconsistency kills a solo-founder
   raise faster than any market risk. → #779, #780, #787

## Strategy (CSO)

The game being played is not invoice OCR — that is a commodity, and the plan says so
itself. It is a race to own the gestoría channel and the ingredient-price dataset before
Haddock or a generic (Holded, Quipu) bolts on line-item extraction. On that game we are
directionally right and operationally under-armed.

**Strong:** counter-positioning is real — public pricing, self-service and
minutes-to-value directly weaponize the incumbents' worst-reviewed traits (the
`competitor_xtrachef.md` teardown is excellent channel intelligence). The gestoría logic
mirrors xtraCHEF's accountant segment, and Quipu/Holded prove the partner mechanics.

**Risks, ranked:**

1. **Distribution, full stop.** 30%→60% of signups assumed via a channel with zero
   partners signed. Haddock took four years and €1M to reach ~2,000 restaurants *with* a
   commercial team.
2. **The 27-Aug pivot breaks two planks of the investor story.** We cannot pitch
   payment-state reporting as the RD 238/2026 wedge while deleting payment states from
   the UX. VERI\*FACTU binds *issuers*; the product is explicitly not a SIF. The tailwind
   is largely narrative today.
3. **Moat claims are futures.** The price graph needs ~1,000 restaurants with zone
   density; `extraction_corrections` is an eval set, not a defensible dataset. Current
   defensibility = counter-positioning + speed. Say that plainly; investors respect it.
4. **The xtraCHEF ceiling.** The closest analogue exited for ~$49M to a POS after six
   years. Be honest about which outcome €750k underwrites.

**Also recommends:** narrow the beachhead to Barcelona only ("Madrid + Barcelona, direct"
is two fronts, not a beachhead); defer TPV scraping and ship only the CSV/Z-report
fallback; consider inverting the 45/35 product/GTM split toward a senior commercial hire.

## Finance (CFO)

Fundable in structure, un-investable in one specific way: the deck prices at
€49/€99/€199 while `src/lib/billing-plans.ts:3` ships €29/€59/€129. At provisional
prices, blended ARPA ≈ €45 rather than €65–75, Y1 ARR ≈ €70k rather than €117k, and
LTV/CAC drops from 7× to ≈ 4.4×.

**Holds up:** billing is genuinely built, not a stub — Stripe checkout with idempotency
keys, signature-verified deduped webhooks, fails-closed trials, atomic quota claims.
COGS math is credible (<€0.01/doc). The Y2 burn multiple ≈ 1.0 is good, and the §9
downside sensitivity is one founders usually omit.

**Risks:** the price divergence (#780); ≤3% churn asserted against the plan's own
citation that 6 of 10 new restaurants close within two years; conversion machinery with
zero live Stripe transactions ever (#785); runway tight at ~18–20 months with an 8%
cushion; and the payment-tracking drop staling the €49 tier's bullet list (#787).

## Product (CPO)

Ship the beta — but ship a smaller product than the one we have built. QA verified the
review UX, per-field confidence, duplicate detection and end-to-end Facturae parsing. The
real problem is identity: the 27-Aug decision is correct and is currently woven against
the grain of roughly a third of the UI. Entering beta half-pivoted means testers learn a
product we have already decided to kill, and every piece of feedback arrives corrupted.

The pivot itself is cheap — the design doc concludes *"renombrar y quitar, no migrar"*:
near-zero data-model debt, all UI and copy debt, with QA having already enumerated every
instance. → #782

**Also:** supplier-level categories leave ~25% of spend uncategorised (#790), and
time-to-value is a headline claim that nothing measures (#786).

**Freeze for beta:** recipes, stock, budgets, multi-location. **Do not start** PO
matching (#25), gestoría portal, or the e-invoice module until 10+ beta restaurants show
a week-4 review habit. Then sequence: gestoría *export* → PO matching → e-invoice timed
to the Oct-2027 window.

## Marketing (CMO)

The strategy documents are investable; the channel evidence is not yet. The load-bearing
gestoría assumption has had zero conversations (#783), Phase 1's SEO/content engine is
marked *sin empezar*, and the flagship price-index magnet is circular — it needs the
hundreds of restaurants it was supposed to attract (#330).

**Strong:** positioning done right — the true competitive alternative named as *"la
carpeta de plástico y la hoja de cálculo"*, one ICP, public pricing as a wedge against
consultative incumbents. Message-integrity infrastructure (never claim VERI\*FACTU
compliance, no unsourced figures, plus a landing-claims regression test) that most funded
startups lack at Series B.

**Risks:** the channel's own disqualifying objections (typing hours are gestoría
*revenue*; despachos already partner with Holded/Quipu; we export Excel, not
a3/Contasol); trust debts on the only public asset (#788); Kit Digital as a slide rather
than a plan; and measurement blindness (#786).

**Resequence content:** normativa pieces against the 2027 calendar now; the price index
becomes a month-12+ PR asset.

## Operations (COO)

Go for a 50-restaurant beta, conditional on an external worker alert (#781) and the
staging smoke pass with live keys (#785) — because the five systems QA did not cover are
precisely the ones touching money and the primary ingestion path.

**Solid:** 241 test files with CI against live Postgres that hard-fails skipped DB
suites; idempotent job design with claim-before-do, dedup and redacted dead-letter
queues; the stall reaper deliberately living on the web path so a dead worker's stuck
uploads still surface to the user. The docs are the best bus-factor mitigation available
at this stage.

**What breaks first:** at 50 — a silent worker outage (#781) or a WhatsApp ban (#789);
both detection/dependency problems, not load. At 500 — the shared WhatsApp number,
morning extraction latency (#791), and Gemini spend visibility, in that order. The
Railway config cutoff on 2026-12-01 (#740) is a scheduled outage with a known date.

**Measure weekly:** worker time-to-detect-down (<5 min), extraction p95 queue-to-done,
dead-letter count per 24h, Gemini € per tenant.

## Legal (GC)

Strong privacy engineering on a legal foundation that does not exist. The privacy policy
and terms name "Mise en Place SL" as controller and IP owner; ADR-025 states there is no
registered business. A non-existent SL cannot be a GDPR controller, cannot execute the
SCCs the policy asserts, and leaves the founder personally and unlimitedly liable —
including for the deliberately-accepted WhatsApp ToS breach. **This gates the first
external tester.** → #779

**In decent shape:** the 20-point security audit is near-fully green; data-subject rights
are real code (export and deletion, transactional, with Stripe/file cleanup); consent is
versioned per signup; and the regulatory-claims discipline (MDR-001: never claim
VERI\*FACTU compliance) is the right position — protect it.

**Then:** the processor-role gap — no Art. 28 DPA annex, unexecuted vendor DPAs, an
unnamed Railway transfer basis, and undisclosed WhatsApp ingestion (#784). Confirm Gemini
is on the **paid tier with Google's DPA accepted**; free-tier Gemini may train on prompts,
which would contradict the policy. EU AI Act exposure is low — document the classification
and move on (#792).

*Executive-level legal framing, not legal advice. The binding calls need a licensed
Spanish attorney.*
